An events app for a co-op coming home.

MEC Connect

What if your next outdoor adventure started with a new friendship? Built with Mountain Equipment Company to rebuild community engagement.

02 / The problem

A co-op that lost
its sense of community.

MEC was founded as a Canadian co-operative rooted in environmental values and community. In 2020 US-based Kingswood Capital acquired it over loud member protest. In 2025 Canadian owners 3F Capital bought it back.

Three eras, two ruptures, and a lingering trust gap with the people who used to call MEC theirs.

03 / What we heard

Eleven conversations,
three patterns.

Retail customers, store staff, and longtime members.

Insight 01

A legacy trust gap

Long-time customers and ex-co-op members felt MEC had lost its mythology, community, and identity through the corporate years.

Insight 02

Beginners had nowhere to start

Newcomers felt overwhelmed and unsure where to begin, despite MEC’s reputation as a place that welcomes them.

Insight 03

A strong but hidden community

Loyal customers existed, but lacked visibility or any real way to connect with each other.

“We host events to keep people connected. Community is a big part of what MEC stands for.”

MEC store staff · our north star
04 / The solution

MEC Connect, an events app
that puts community first.

Events are how MEC’s community actually shows up. But they were buried, hard to find on the website, easy to miss on Instagram, invisible in store. MEC Connect is a dedicated events app.

  • Discover

    Surfaced by location, community, and adventure type, with QR entry from in-store signage.

  • Connect before the event

    Opt-in group chat with other attendees, so newcomers don’t show up alone.

  • Earn & belong

    Fire Safety, Tent Technician, and Hiking Expert badges, tied to MEC’s heritage rather than points farming.

  • Community feed

    Ambassadors, announcements, and post-event posts that make MEC’s hidden community visible again.

05 / In use

Spike’s first event,
step by step.

Spike just moved to Vancouver. He’s an avid trail runner who knows no locals.

He scans a QR code at his nearest MEC store, finds the We Run the Cold event, RSVPs, and opts into the pre-event group chat, arriving with new friends already lined up.

What I took
from MEC.

Designing alongside MEC showed me how product decisions become stronger when they are informed by real stakeholder context, not just assumptions.

Every choice had to earn its place against MEC’s brand identity, feasibility, and how community actually forms around events.
