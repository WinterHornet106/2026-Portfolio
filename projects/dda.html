2024 · Microsite & posters

Giving young designers the main stage.

Dutch Design Awards

A poster series and event microsite that spotlights the Young Designers category at the Dutch Design Awards.

Role
Visual designer
Year
2024
Team
Student brief, 5 designers at SFU
Surface
Posters, web prototype

Eight categories,
one overlooked.

The Dutch Design Awards span eight categories. Young Designers is different: nominees submit a whole portfolio, the category comes with a grant, and it’s often overlooked.

We designed a microsite for the nine-day event that pairs the schedule with the young nominees.

Posters first,
then the web.

We built the visual system on posters first, exploring three directions from Ellen Lupton’s design principles. Early rounds were too literal; the final three balanced image, type and space.

A functional grid,
not an expressive one.

We tried an expressive layout against a functional one. The functional grid matched the posters and gave us a clearer direction, so the whole site runs on it.

Key interactions

Three posters,
one working site.

Every page carries the poster system: a homepage, nominees by year, every past nominee, the nine-day schedule and the category’s history.

What I owned

Research

  • Client research on the Dutch Design Awards and its categories
  • Exhibition research into the nine-day Dutch Design Week event

Posters

  • Poster explorations across the lines of investigation

Microsite

  • Microsite screens in the functional grid system

Looking back

A decision I pushed for

I designed the microsite screens in the functional grid. We tried an expressive layout too, but the functional one matched the posters and gave the site a clearer direction, so every page runs on it.

What I’d do next

Test the site with people planning a Dutch Design Week visit, especially whether the poster grid still reads on a phone. Our posters started too literal and too conceptual; a round of outside feedback earlier would have got us to the final three sooner.
